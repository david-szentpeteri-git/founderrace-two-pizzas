## Boilerplates: what's allowed

Several of you asked: **yes, you can start from a boilerplate or a template**, including one you built yourself. Four conditions (article 7 of the rules):

1. **It's public.** Any other participant must be able to access it.
2. **It's generic.** Infrastructure only (auth, payments, database, UI components...), never business logic, mockups or content tied to your future product.
3. **Fresh repo on race day.** Create your repository **after the start**, then import the boilerplate in a commit made after the start (or with "Use this template" on GitHub). Don't reuse the boilerplate's history: a commit dated before the start looks like prior work.
4. **It's credited in the README.** Include the boilerplate's link **and the exact commit** you used.

The jury may compare your repo with the boilerplate you credit: what counts is what you build during the 24 hours.

Questions? Ask them here before the start. (Sylvain)
